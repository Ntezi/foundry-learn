# Lesson 4 — Context: Fresh Air

## Operational setting

**Fresh Air** is a fictional regional airline with about 4,500 employees. It operates roughly 500 flights per day to 83 destinations, with its busiest hubs on the eastern coast of the United States. The course begins from the perspective of a new Flight Operations employee.

## The airline Ontology

The Ontology converts industry vocabulary into connected operational objects:

- An **Airline** operates a fleet of **Aircraft**.
- An **Aircraft** flies between **Airports**.
- A **Runway** is where an aircraft takes off or lands at an airport.
- A **Route** connects two airports that aircraft regularly travel between.
- A **Flight** is a specific aircraft travelling a route at a particular date and time.
- A **Flight Alert** represents a non-standard event such as a delay, cancellation, or diversion that Flight Operations must address.

The important idea is that the relationships add operational meaning. A table of flights alone does not explain the surrounding airline, aircraft, route, airports, runways, and alerts. The Ontology makes those connections directly navigable.

The installed training object types use the **OFT** prefix, meaning **Ontology Foundry Training**, to avoid naming collisions with object types that may already exist in the organization.
