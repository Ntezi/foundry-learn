# 03 — Business Elements

*Module: Use Case Prioritization Elements · Inspected September 23, 2026 · Notes: Ready for Review*

## Four business factors

### 1. Problem statement
Summarize **what you want to achieve**, **where the current process has gaps**, and **how this differs from today**: either there is no solution, or the current one doesn't work well.

> **Fit for Foundry:** the best Foundry-shaped problems are often **loosely defined**. You don't need a precise solution yet, but you do need conviction about the **pain it causes users, and why**. Users' drive to reach a solution fast is a key success factor.

### 2. Alignment with company objectives
Is the project instrumental to the organization's strategic goals and current trends? What is the **core business problem**?

> **Fit for Foundry:** impact often **crosses several areas of the organization**, especially through data. Reusing an existing data asset, or creating one that will outlive this use case, is a sign of a strong Foundry use case.

### 3. Return on investment (ROI)
Ideally a dollar value. Realistically, use ballpark estimates, the **number of users affected**, and the **teams or business units whose way of working changes**. If you **can't** say anything about ROI, that should lower the priority.

> **Fit for Foundry:** **sharp user pain** is a strong signal. Look for slow processes, no end-to-end connectivity or traceability, and friction between teams. These translate into headcount affected, time saved and regulatory commitments met.

### 4. Urgency
A deadline shows priority. If there is none, ask what happens if the project **isn't** done, and what other initiatives compete with it.

> **Fit for Foundry:** tight deadlines, pressure to reach production, and the need to **iterate in production** are clear priority signals. Foundry's **production-ready-by-default** environment is designed to support them.

The lesson includes two illustrations: example problems (integrating new sources, transforming or generating information) and value for users.

**Remember:** Problem · Alignment · ROI · Urgency. A loosely defined problem with sharp pain and urgency is very "Foundry-shaped".

**Review question:** a team can't name any benefit of a proposed use case, not even the number of users affected. What does the course advise? Lower its priority.
