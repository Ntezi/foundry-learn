# 04 — Team or People Elements

*Module: Use Case Prioritization Elements · Inspected September 23, 2026 · Notes: Ready for Review*

## Why the team matters for priority

A structured project team, with clear roles and **focal points** for specific responsibilities, helps you judge a use case's **urgency and feasibility** (and so its priority). It also makes delivery smoother.

## Three team factors

| Factor | What to check | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Business availability** | Can the **users** commit time? They should be at the center of the use case | If they can't, maybe first tackle whatever is taking up their time |
| **Technical resources availability** | Is anyone available to scope and build? | Willingness to **pull people off other projects** shows real priority |
| **Steering team & project governance** | Is there a steering group to unblock, advocate and secure resources (people, data, money)? | Less of a blocker at kickoff, but it's what moves a **prototype into production**. If nobody will take this on, question the project's feasibility |

> **Fit for Foundry:** nothing replaces **desk-side discussions** with users to understand the problem beyond the first, often narrow, scope. Users willing to iterate in the platform are key: **build fast, test, evolve with the users**.

These don't have to be full-time roles, and one person can hold several.

**Remember:** users' time, builders' time, sponsors' backing.

**Review question:** which team factor is least likely to block the kickoff but most needed to reach production? A steering team and project governance.
