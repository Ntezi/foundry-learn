# 03 — The Evolution from Linear to Decision-Centric Systems

*Module: Course Introduction - Why Foundry & AIP? · Inspected September 23, 2026 · Notes: Ready for Review*

## The assembly-line model and its gaps

Most organizations treat data like an assembly line: producers collect it, integration teams combine it, transformation teams reshape it, reporting teams build dashboards, and decision-makers consume the result. The course accepts that this works up to a point, then names three gaps:

1. **Learning isn't captured back.** What is learned while processing data rarely flows back into the system.
2. **Decisions don't become actions.** Someone sees on a dashboard that a shipment should be rerouted, then has to "swivel-chair" into a different system to make it happen.
3. **No feedback loop** between front-line operators, who see what is really happening, and the analysts producing reports.

## The decision-centric alternative

Foundry and AIP are positioned as a **decision-centric operating system**, not just a data platform: data comes in, decisions emerge, actions are taken, and the results feed back to improve later decisions.

## Engineering comparison

Think of the linear model as a one-way ETL-to-BI pipeline, and the decision-centric model as a control loop: sense → decide → act → observe the effect → adjust. The difference is the write path and the feedback.

**Remember:** "swivel-chair" = acting in a separate system from the one where you saw the insight. The platform's answer is to act where you decide.

**Review question:** name the three gaps of linear data flows. No capture of learnings, decisions not materialized into actions, no operator ↔ analytics feedback loop.
