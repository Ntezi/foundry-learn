# Foundry Speedruns

A guided collection of five Palantir Foundry speedruns, with detailed notes for a software engineer learning Foundry for the first time. Each course explains the purpose behind the steps, how the concepts relate to software development, and what evidence to check during practice.

[Study plan](../STUDY-PLAN.md) · [Progress tracker](../PROGRESS.md)

## Choose a course

The order below builds from an end-to-end workflow to reusable logic, AI workflows, automation and process investigation. Open a course to find its lesson index and supporting files.

| Course | What you will learn | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| [Your First End-to-End Workflow](Your%20First%20End-to-End%20Workflow/README.md) | Connect data preparation, the Ontology and an operational application. | Understand how the parts of Foundry work together before studying them individually. |
| [Your First Ontology Function](Your%20First%20Ontology%20Function/README.md) | Work with Ontology objects through reusable function logic. | Connect your programming background to Foundry's business objects and application behavior. |
| [Your First AIP Workflow](Your%20First%20AIP%20Workflow/README.md) | Prepare information for an AI-assisted workflow and use it in an application. | Understand how data, context and model output fit into a useful business workflow. |
| [Your First Agentic AIP Workflow](Your%20First%20Agentic%20AIP%20Workflow/README.md) | Connect AI logic, evaluation, Actions and automation. | Learn how to assess behavior and distinguish a successful manual test from verified automatic execution. |
| [Mining Your First Business Process](Mining%20Your%20First%20Business%20Process/README.md) | Reconstruct a process from event histories, investigate it in Workshop and configure monitoring. | Use operational data to examine delays and unusual paths, then connect findings to individual cases. |

## How to work through a speedrun

1. **Start with its README.** Understand the business question, the course scope and the lesson sequence.
2. **Read the lesson notes in order.** Focus on why each resource or configuration is needed. Use the examples and troubleshooting guidance to connect the steps to concepts you already know.
3. **Use the review guides.** Explain the workflow in your own words and work through the questions. Return to any lesson where the reasoning is still unclear.
4. **Move to practice after understanding review.** Follow the course's practice preparation and retain evidence of the actual results. Reading instructions is different from demonstrating that the workflow works.
5. **Record progress centrally.** Use the [progress tracker](../PROGRESS.md) for review status, practical results and blockers. Keep study sessions within the planned two-hour daily limit.

The first- and second-half review guides provide manageable checkpoints. Their boundaries follow the course material and may not divide the lesson count equally. Detailed beginner study can take longer than the advertised speedrun duration.

## Find the right file

Each course has its own folder directly under `Speedrun`.

| File or material | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | Course orientation and links to the lessons. |
| Numbered lesson notes | Detailed explanations, source links, examples and lesson-specific questions. |
| Review guides | Connecting concepts across lessons and checking your understanding. |
| Practice guidance | Preparing the hands-on exercise and identifying evidence of success. |
| Source and correction notes, where provided | Distinguishing the official walkthrough from teaching additions, ambiguities and corrections. |
| Course assets, where provided | Supporting the exercise; the End-to-End Workflow folder includes CSV files. |

File names and lesson structures can vary where the material benefits from a different presentation. Keep related files with their course rather than creating duplicate course folders or progress trackers.
